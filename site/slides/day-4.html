01

The generative family tree

Learn p(x) well enough to sample from it. Five ways to do it. One family now rules images.

The art is p(x) itself: a kernel density estimate of today's honeycomb dataset. That glowing shape is what every model this morning is trying to learn.
The map

Many roads from noise to data

Walk left to right. Autoregressive: exact likelihood, great for discrete sequences, slow for long outputs (Day 3). VAE: fast but blurry alone; its modern job is compression. GAN: sharp but unstable, prone to mode collapse; survives as a loss for few-step distillation. Diffusion → flow matching is the line we follow today. Question for the room: which branch generated the last AI image you saw? (Almost certainly the black box.)
VAE · Kingma & Welling 2013

Squeeze through a tiny latent

$$\log p(x)\ge\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x|z)]}_{\text{reconstruct}}-\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,p(z))}_{\text{stay near the prior}}$$
  • Reparameterize: $z=\mu+\sigma\odot\epsilon$, so gradients flow through the sample.
  • Smooth latent: walk through it and 1 → 7 → 9.
  • Alone: blurry. Today's job: the compressor.
Point at the grid: moving along z₁ morphs 1 into 7 into 9 into 0. That smoothness is the KL term at work. Then the twist: a 2-D latent is too small to be sharp, and VAE samples are blurry on their own. But a VAE with a modest compression ratio is an excellent compressor, and that's its job inside Stable Diffusion. (Trains in seconds on a CPU: final reconstruction ≈ 20 nats, KL ≈ 3.5 nats.)
🍯 Key takeaway

Every generator answers one question: how do I turn something simple into the data?

Autoregressive: one piece at a time. VAE: decode a latent. GAN: one adversarial leap. Diffusion & flows: many small, learned steps.

02

Learning to undo noise

Diffusion, then flow matching: the same picture with a simpler path.

The art: the exact flow-matching velocity field at t = 0.75 for the honeycomb. The white dots are where the data sits at that time. Every streamline is a road that a noise sample can take.
DDPM · Ho et al. 2020

Destroy slowly, learn to undo

$$x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$$$$\mathcal{L}=\big\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\big\|^2$$
  • Jump to any noise level in one line.
  • Predicting ε ≈ estimating the score $\nabla_x\log p_t$.
  • Heads-up: DDPM counts backwards.
ᾱ_t is the fraction of signal variance left at step t. Linear schedule: half the signal is gone by t ≈ 259, and the last ~400 steps are nearly pure noise, which wastes steps; that's why Nichol & Dhariwal proposed the cosine schedule. Predicting the noise is, up to a −1/σ_t scale, estimating the score (Song et al. 2021). Warn them: DDPM numbers time from data (0) to noise (T); flow matching, and the rest of today, goes from noise (0) to data (1).
Flow matching · Lipman et al. 2023 · Liu et al. 2023

Draw a straight line, regress its velocity

$$x_t=(1-t)\,x_0+t\,x_1\qquad \mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,x_1}\big\|\,v_\theta(x_t,t)-(x_1-x_0)\,\big\|^2$$
x₀ ∼ 𝒩(0, I)noise, at t = 0
x₁ ∼ pdataa real sample, at t = 1
x₁ − x₀the target: where you're going minus where you started
dx/dt = vθsampling: integrate from t = 0 to 1
This is the most important slide of the morning. Differentiate x_t with respect to t and you get x₁ − x₀: that's the whole target. No noise schedule, no simulation during training, plain MSE. Ask: "Why doesn't the network just learn x₁ − x₀ exactly?" Because from x_t alone it can't tell which pair it came from, so it learns the average, E[x₁ − x₀ | x_t]. That average is the marginal velocity, and it's what makes the next slide interesting.
The subtle part

Straight in training, curved at sampling

Training lines cross; an ODE's paths can't. So the learned field averages every pair through a point, and its paths bend. (Right: the exact marginal field, computed.)
Left: what the loss sees, random (noise, data) pairs connected by straight lines that cross everywhere. Right: what sampling follows, the marginal field. A deterministic ODE can't have two paths through one point, so the paths swerve. Curved paths are why one big Euler step fails, which is the quiz coming up.
The whole algorithm

Train and sample in eleven lines

for x1 in loader:                       # data batch
    x0 = torch.randn_like(x1)           # noise batch
    t  = torch.rand(len(x1), 1)
    xt = (1 - t) * x0 + t * x1          # on the line
    loss = ((model(xt, t) - (x1 - x0))**2).mean()
    opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()

x = torch.randn(n, 2)                   # Euler: t 0 → 1
for i in range(steps):
    t = torch.full((n, 1), i / steps)
    x = x + model(x, t) / steps
No simulationTraining never runs the ODE.
No schedulet ∼ U(0, 1), straight path.
Sampling = ODEEuler here; Heun in the lab.
This is essentially the Lab 4.1 training loop. Point out that a real image model changes only "model" (a DiT) and "x1" (VAE latents). Heun = Euler plus one correction step: roughly twice the cost per step, much better accuracy for curved paths.

Reflow · rectified flow

Straighten the paths, then take one step

Real: bottom row is an MLP trained by flow matching on the top row's own (noise → sample) pairs. Bend = mean ‖ẋ − (x₁ − x₀)‖².
Top: the exact flow (a perfectly trained network). One step collapses to the mean; two steps give a blob; four steps begin to show hexagons. Bottom: reflow. Run the top ODE to pair each noise point with the sample it produces, then train a new flow on those pairs. The new pairs don't cross, so the paths are nearly straight (bend 0.85 → 0.001) and one step gives a crisp honeycomb. Distillation and consistency models push the same idea further for real images. Trains on a laptop CPU.
🍯 Key takeaway

Training is regression. Sampling is ODE solving. Straighter paths, fewer steps.

DDPM, score models and flow matching are one family: a time-dependent field carrying noise to data. Flow matching just picks the simplest path.

03

Steering with guidance

One network, two predictions, and a knob that trades variety for fidelity.

Art: the same noise cloud, guided (w = 3) toward each of three classes with exact class-conditional flows. Each color is one condition.
Classifier-free guidance · Ho & Salimans 2022

Push away from “anything”, toward the prompt

$$\tilde\epsilon=(1+w)\,\epsilon_\theta(x,t,c)\;-\;w\,\epsilon_\theta(x,t,\varnothing)$$

Exact class-conditional flows of a Gaussian mixture; the same formula applied to velocities. Train with labels dropped ~10% of the time so one net learns both terms.

Read the formula: w = 0 is the plain conditional model, w = −1 is unconditional. Larger w extrapolates away from the unconditional prediction. Left to right: on-class rate goes 33% → 90% → 100%, spread shrinks 0.99 → 0.34, and at w = 8 samples overshoot past the class mean: that's 2-D over-saturation. Label dropout (~10%) is what lets one network give both ε(x, c) and ε(x, ∅). Note: some papers write the scale as s = 1 + w.
🍯 Key takeaway

Guidance is a knob, not a dial: every notch buys fidelity with diversity.

Tune w per model and per prompt. Max it out and samples over-saturate and lose variety. Lab 4.2 lets you feel it.

04

Scaling up: latents & DiT

Compress the image, patchify the latent, hand it to a transformer.

Art: this morning's cover cut into patches, a few lifted out as "tokens". That's literally what a DiT sees.
Latent diffusion · Rombach et al. 2022

Anatomy of a text-to-image model

Encode once, 8× smaller per side: 1024² × 3 = 3.1 M numbers become 128² × 16 = 262 k. Every denoising step works on ~12× fewer numbers; the decoder adds texture once at the end.
Walk the pipeline. Prompt → text encoder gives conditioning tokens. Start from a noise latent, run the DiT N times (Euler/Heun on the velocity), decode once. The 16-channel latent matches SD3-style autoencoders; SD 1.x used 4 channels at 8× (64×64×4 for a 512² image). Pixels are mostly redundant high-frequency detail: spend compute on semantics, let the decoder handle texture. Video: same recipe with spacetime patches; add action conditioning and you get interactive world models.
DiT · Peebles & Xie 2023

The UNet becomes a transformer

  • Patches → tokens → plain transformer blocks.
  • adaLN-Zero: t and c set each block's scale, shift and gate; gates start at 0.
  • Scales like an LLM: more compute, better FID.
  • SD3: rectified flow + MM-DiT, text and image tokens in one attention.
adaLN-Zero: an MLP maps the timestep (and class or pooled text) embedding to per-block γ, β and a gate α; the gate is initialized to zero, so each residual block starts as the identity, which stabilizes training. Peebles & Xie showed FID improves steadily with Gflops, which is why everyone switched. SD3 (Esser et al. 2024) runs rectified flow with an MM-DiT whose text and image tokens attend to each other. Video models use spacetime patches.
🍯 Key takeaway

Compress first, then denoise: spend compute on meaning, not pixels.

The VAE handles texture. The DiT handles semantics, and it scales like the GPT you built yesterday.

05

Pictures meet words

CLIP builds one space for images and text. VLMs read from it.

Art: the similarity matrix of 28 held-out image–caption pairs from the toy model on the next slide. The bright diagonal means each image found its own caption.
CLIP · Radford et al. 2021

Two towers, one space

  • Image tower f, text tower g.
  • N right pairs, N²−N wrong.
  • Softmax over rows and columns.
  • SigLIP: one sigmoid per pair.
The negatives are free: every other caption in the batch. Bigger batch = more and harder negatives, which is why CLIP used 32k. The temperature τ is learned (ours went from 0.07 to ~0.03). SigLIP (Zhai et al. 2023) scores each pair with an independent sigmoid, so it doesn't need a batch-wide softmax and scales better across devices. Lab 4.3 implements both losses.
Vision-language models · the LLaVA recipe

How a VLM sees

Zero-shot CLIP needs no LLM: embed “a photo of a {class}” for every class and pick the nearest. Newer natively multimodal models train on interleaved image + text from the start.
LLaVA-style: a pretrained vision encoder turns the image into patch embeddings, a small projector maps them into the LLM's embedding space, and the LLM reads them as "visual tokens" next to the text. Often only the projector (then the LLM) is trained. Zero-shot classification: prompt wording matters; ensembling several templates ("a photo of a…", "a blurry photo of a…") reliably helps. Ask: what would you change to make the VLM generate images too?
🍯 Key takeaway

Contrastive learning turns classification into retrieval: name the class in words, pick the nearest.

The negatives are free: every other pair in the batch. That shared space is also how text steers an image generator.
