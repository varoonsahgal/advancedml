The through-line

One idea, five disguises.

Underneath everything this week is the same loop: define what "good" means, measure the gradient of it, and take a step. What changes each day is what you differentiate and what you step through.

Who it's for

Practitioners leveling up

Data scientists, ML engineers, researchers, and ambitious students who have trained a model or two and want to know how modern ML actually works.

You should know

Prerequisites

  • Comfortable Python + numpy
  • Basic PyTorch (tensors, nn.Module, a training loop)
  • Linear algebra and probability at the undergrad level
  • Some ML: train/test splits, overfitting, a classifier or two

  • Opinions about learning rates (sorry)
The hive manifesto

Ten lessons you'll carry home.

The week has hundreds of details. These are the ten principles underneath them, and they'll still be true in 2030.

01

Look before you model.

Plot the data, read 20 raw examples, and check the labels. Most "model bugs" are data bugs.

02

Beat a strong baseline first.

On tables that means a GBDT; for LLM apps, a good prompt. Fancy only counts if it wins.

03

Validate like a skeptic.

Held-out data, no leakage, several seeds. Report the variance, not just your best run.

04

Predictions need error bars.

Calibrate probabilities and conformalize intervals. Uncertainty is a feature.

05

Blame the learning rate first.

Then warmup, clipping and normalization. Most "it won't train" problems end here.

06

Small runs predict big ones.

Scaling laws turn guesses into forecasts. Fit the curve before you buy the cluster.

07

Inference is the product.

Latency, memory and cost per token are design constraints, not afterthoughts.

08

Generation is transport.

From noise to images, from prompts to answers: learn the flow, then steer it.

09

Evals before optimization.

If you can't measure it, you're not improving it, just changing it.

10

Inputs are data, not orders.

Anything an agent reads may be adversarial. Least privilege, confirmation, and isolation.
