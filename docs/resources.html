Speak fluent ML

Glossary

Bias–varianceSplit of expected error into systematic error (bias), sensitivity to the training sample (variance), and irreducible noise.
Double descentTest error that peaks at the interpolation threshold, then falls again as capacity grows.
Gradient boostingAn additive ensemble in which each weak learner fits the negative gradient (pseudo-residuals) of the loss.
ECEExpected calibration error: the size-weighted gap between confidence and accuracy across bins.
Conformal predictionA wrapper that turns any model into prediction sets or intervals with finite-sample coverage guarantees.
Shapley valueA feature's average marginal contribution across all coalitions; the basis of SHAP.
TabPFNA transformer pretrained on synthetic tables that predicts new tabular tasks in context.
Reverse-mode ADBackpropagation: gradients of one output with respect to all inputs at about the cost of one extra pass.
RMSNormNormalization by root-mean-square only (no mean-centering), standard in modern LLMs.
AdamWAdam with weight decay applied directly to the weights rather than through the loss.
MuonAn optimizer that orthogonalizes the momentum of 2-D weight matrices with Newton–Schulz iterations.
WSD scheduleWarmup–stable–decay: a flat learning rate with a short cooldown at the end.
GrokkingGeneralization that arrives long after the model has memorized the training set.
Scaling lawA power-law relationship between loss and parameters, data or compute.
RoPERotary position embedding: rotates q and k so attention depends on relative position.
SwiGLUA gated MLP, SiLU(W₁x) ⊙ W₃x, then W₂.
GQA / MQAGrouped- or multi-query attention: query heads share K/V heads, shrinking the KV cache.
KV cacheStored keys and values of past tokens, reused at each decoding step.
FlashAttentionAn exact attention kernel that tiles through on-chip memory to avoid materializing the T×T matrix.
Speculative decodingA draft model proposes tokens and the target model verifies them in parallel, with exact output distribution.
Top-p / min-pTruncation samplers: the smallest set with mass ≥ p, or tokens above p × the max probability.
Mixture-of-expertsA router sends each token to a few expert MLPs: more parameters at the same FLOPs per token.
State-space modelA linear recurrence with a fixed-size state; Mamba makes its parameters input-dependent.
ELBOEvidence lower bound: reconstruction minus KL to the prior; the VAE objective.
DiffusionGeneration by learning to reverse a gradual noising process.
Flow matchingRegressing a velocity field along simple noise→data paths, then sampling by solving an ODE.
Rectified flow / reflowStraightening flow paths by re-pairing noise with generated samples, enabling few-step sampling.
CFGClassifier-free guidance: extrapolating from unconditional toward conditional predictions.
DiTDiffusion transformer: a transformer denoiser over latent patches.
InfoNCEA contrastive loss that classifies the true pair among in-batch negatives; the CLIP objective.
LoRA / QLoRALow-rank adapters on frozen weights; QLoRA keeps the frozen base in 4-bit.
DPODirect preference optimization: preference learning without a reward model or RL loop.
RLVRRL from verifiable rewards: rewards from automatic checkers (tests, exact answers).
GRPOGroup relative policy optimization: PPO-style updates with advantages normalized within a group of samples.
Test-time computeSpending more inference (longer reasoning, sampling, voting, search) to improve answers.
RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: fetch relevant documents and condition the answer on them.
MCPModel Context Protocol: an open standard for exposing tools, resources and prompts to AI applications.
Prompt injectionMalicious instructions hidden in content an LLM reads, aiming to hijack its behaviour.
SuperpositionRepresenting more features than dimensions using nearly orthogonal directions.
Sparse autoencoderAn overcomplete, sparsity-penalized autoencoder used to extract interpretable features from activations.
Running the course

Instructor notes

Notes for whoever is leading the hive.

🗓️ Pacing

  • Lectures are 45–60 minutes. Stop at 60 even when you're on a roll; the labs are where people learn.
  • Every lab has a natural "minimum viable" stopping point before the 🏆 Boss Level. Tell people it's fine to stop there.
  • Fast finishers: point them at Boss Level challenges or the capstone, not the next lab.
  • Run the morning quiz from the previous day as a warm-up. Retrieval practice works.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Room setup

  • Pair programming: driver and navigator, swapping at each ✔️ checkpoint.
  • One TA per ~10 learners. TAs circulate during labs and don't lecture.
  • Put up a "parking lot" board for deep questions to answer at the end of the day.
  • Keep the playground open on a big screen during lectures for live demos.

✂️ Shorter formats

  • 3 days: Day 1 (1.1, 1.2) → Day 2+3 merged (2.1, 3.1, 3.2) → Day 4+5 merged (4.1, 5.1, 5.3).
  • Self-paced: one day per week over five weeks, with the capstone as a final project.
  • LLM-focused: skip Day 1 and add Labs 5.3/5.4 together.

🧯 Common snags

  • Colab disconnects: save a copy to Drive first and checkpoint models to disk.
  • HF download throttling: have learners log in with huggingface-cli login or set a token.
  • Slow CPU runs: LAB_FAST=1 shrinks everything for demos.
  • No API key for Lab 5.3: the built-in MockLLM keeps the whole loop runnable.
Questions

FAQ

Do I need a GPU?

No. Every lab runs on CPU (Colab's free tier is fine). Labs marked ⚡ are faster on a free T4 GPU and produce prettier results, especially diffusion (4.2), LoRA (5.1) and GRPO (5.2).

How much math do I need?

Comfort with derivatives, matrix multiplication, and expectations and variances. Each concept is introduced with intuition first, and the equations are annotated.

Why implement things from scratch when libraries exist?

Because you can't debug, tune or extend what you don't understand. Each lab ends by comparing your version with the production library, so you leave knowing both.

Can I use this material for my own course?

Yes. Fork the repo, change site/ and notebooks/, and rebuild with python scripts/build.py. Please keep attribution to the original papers in the reading lists.

Why a bee?

Bees are distributed, gradient-following optimizers that turn noise (flowers everywhere!) into structure (honeycomb). Also, yellow is a very pleasant color to read on. 🐝
